## The problem

VPs of Product Marketing are asked to move fast, but major messaging decisions often still depend on internal opinion. The tension is not "do research or do not research." It is whether the team can get credible outside-in evidence fast enough to use it before the launch, rewrite, or board review.

## Campaign idea

**The PMM Confidence Index**

A short benchmark that asks senior B2B marketers seven questions about how they validate messaging, how often internal stakeholders disagree, and what evidence they trust before changing a page or campaign.

The output is a simple score:

- **0-39: Opinion-led**
- **40-69: Partially validated**
- **70-100: Buyer-grounded**

The hook is not the score itself. The hook is the gap between how confident the team feels and how much direct buyer evidence they actually use.

## LinkedIn hooks

1. Most messaging debates are not copy problems. They are evidence problems.
2. If five smart people in a room agree, that is still not buyer validation.
3. The fastest way to end a positioning argument is to bring in someone who does not work at your company: the buyer.
4. "We know our customers" is not the same as testing the exact message you are about to ship.
5. A confidence score is only useful if it tells you what evidence is missing.
6. The PMM question I would benchmark this quarter: how often does buyer evidence actually change the copy?

## Four-email sequence

**Email 1 - Subject:** Is your messaging evidence-based or consensus-based?  
Introduce the 3-minute benchmark and invite the reader to get a score.

**Email 2 - Subject:** The expensive version of "I think"  
Show how internal disagreement delays launches and weakens differentiation. CTA: take the index.

**Email 3 - Subject:** What strong validation actually looks like  
Share a simple maturity ladder: internal opinion -> sales anecdotes -> customer interviews -> message testing -> ongoing tracking.

**Email 4 - Subject:** Use the score before your next launch  
Invite high-fit respondents to run a real message test against their ICP.

Primary metric: qualified demos created from benchmark participants.

Secondary metrics: benchmark completion rate, share rate, webinar registration, demo conversion by score band, and sourced pipeline.
